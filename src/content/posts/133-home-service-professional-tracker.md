Every homeowner has had the same nightmare: something breaks — a pipe bursts, the garbage disposal dies, the HVAC makes a sound it shouldn't — and suddenly you're Googling "emergency plumber near me" at 10pm, clicking through Yelp ads, and rolling the dice on a stranger who may or may not show up, may or may not charge a reasonable rate, and may or may not actually fix the problem.

The solution isn't complicated. It's just a database of people you've already vetted. But nobody maintains it. It lives in a pile of business cards in a kitchen drawer, or in a text thread with yourself, or nowhere at all.

OpenClaw can maintain that directory for you — automatically, persistently, and usefully.

## The Problem

Home service professionals are hired infrequently and forgotten quickly. You might use a plumber twice a year. An electrician maybe once every three years. A drain cleaner, a roofer, a painter — these relationships are sporadic by nature.

That infrequency is exactly what makes them hard to track:

- **You forget who you used.** Six months later, was it "Mike's Plumbing" or "Apex Plumbing"? The business card is somewhere in the drawer.
- **You lose the contact info.** Phone numbers change. Websites go down. That guy's number was in your old phone.
- **You forget what you paid.** Was the water heater replacement $1,200 or $1,800? Did they charge for the estimate?
- **You don't know who to trust for what.** Your plumber might be great for leak repairs but terrible at installs. You only find out after the fact.
- **Service windows close.** Your HVAC was serviced two years ago. Was that annual maintenance? Did you miss a scheduling window?

The cost of this disorganization is real: higher prices from calling whoever shows up first, duplicate service calls because you forgot what was already done, and the stress of scrambling when something breaks.

## The Solution

OpenClaw maintains a structured directory of every home service professional you've ever hired — plus a scoring system that gets smarter over time.

### How It Works

You build the directory conversationally. When you hire someone, you tell OpenClaw about them:

> "Add to my pros list: Marco the Plumber, licensed, specializes in water heaters and leak repair. Used him twice — fixed the kitchen pipe leak in 2022 ($180) and replaced the water heater in 2023 ($1,400). Showed up on time both times. Phone: 555-0147. I'd use him again for plumbing but probably not for major remodels."

OpenClaw stores this in a structured file and indexes it. Now you can ask:

> "Who do I have for plumbing?"
> "Find me an electrician who does panel upgrades."
> "When was the last time I had the gutters cleaned?"
> "Which HVAC company has the best reviews in my directory?"

### What OpenClaw Tracks

**Contact & Licensing Info**
Business name, phone, email, license number, insurance status, and any certifications. OpenClaw can remind you when a license is due for renewal.

**Service History**
Every job they've done for you — what, when, cost, and outcome. This becomes institutional memory that survives the years between hires.

**Reliability Score**
Track dimensions that matter: punctuality, accuracy of estimate vs. final bill, whether they fixed it right the first time, communication quality, and cleanliness. After each job, OpenClaw can prompt you to rate the experience.

**Specialization Tags**
Not all plumbers are equal. Some are great at emergency calls. Others do beautiful tile work. Some electricians won't touch panel upgrades. Tag accordingly.

**Rate Memory**
OpenClaw tracks what you paid for specific jobs, adjusted for inflation. When you get an estimate, it can tell you: "The last time you replaced a water heater was 2023 for $1,400. That estimate of $2,100 is 50% higher — ask for a breakdown."

## Concrete Examples

### The Emergency Call

It's Saturday night. Your basement drain is backing up.

> "Who do I have for drain cleaning and how were my last two experiences?"

OpenClaw returns: "Drain King — called them in 2024 for a clogged kitchen drain. $140, showed up same day, fixed it in 30 minutes, no follow-up needed. Rating: 5/5 punctuality, 4/5 value. Phone: 555-0293."

You call. You have a vetted professional, not a roll of the dice.

### The Seasonal Reminder

Rainy season is coming. OpenClaw checks your directory and prompts:

> "Your gutters were last cleaned in April 2025 — that's 16 months ago. Drain King (who you rated 4/5) also does gutter cleaning. Want me to ask them for a quote?"

### The Price Check

You get an HVAC estimate for $3,200 to replace a compressor.

> "Based on your directory, the last HVAC company you used was Cool Air Solutions ($2,800 for a comparable unit in 2023). That estimate is 14% higher. I'd ask for a line-item breakdown before signing."

## What You Need to Set It Up

- **A directory file** — OpenClaw can create and maintain this. You just add entries over time.
- **A rating habit** — After any service call, spend 30 seconds rating the experience. OpenClaw can prompt you.
- **A schedule** — Set a monthly or seasonal reminder to review the directory and check on expiring licenses, approaching service intervals, and new needs.

## Limitations

- OpenClaw won't find *new* pros for you — it only tracks who you've already hired. For discovery, combine it with a local review search.
- The data is only as good as what you enter. Garbage in, garbage out — but even a rough directory beats a pile of business cards.
- It doesn't integrate with booking systems. It reminds you; you still make the call.

## The Real Value

Home ownership is partly a project of relationship management with tradespeople. The people who do good work for fair prices are worth their weight in gold. OpenClaw's job isn't glamorous — it's just making sure you never lose them.
