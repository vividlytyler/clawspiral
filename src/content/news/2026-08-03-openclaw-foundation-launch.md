The OpenClaw Foundation has officially launched as a 501(c)(3) American non-profit, marking a decisive moment in the project's journey from weekend hack to global infrastructure. The foundation, announced July 8, 2026, brings the project's governance in line with other enduring open-source projects like Linux, Apache, and Mozilla — and signals that OpenClaw is no longer a side project but a platform organizations rely on.

## What the Foundation Does

The foundation's stated mission: keep OpenClaw MIT-licensed, open, and independent for the long term. It handles governance, funding, and paying the people who maintain the project — functions that became critical as OpenClaw grew to 4.5 million new agents being created every week.

Peter Steinberger, OpenClaw's creator, remains technical lead. Since joining OpenAI earlier in 2026, he has continued to steward OpenClaw as an open and independent project, with OpenAI committing to support the foundation's stewardship.

> "We think the future is personal AI that actually does things. It runs on your machine, works with the apps that you already use, and answers to you and you alone." — OpenClaw Foundation

## The Full-Time Team

For the first time, OpenClaw has paid staff. The initial engineering team includes Vincent Koc as Chief Architect, Josh Avant, Patrick Erichsen, Dallin Romney, Jason Sy, and Gideon Adegbesan. Operations are led by Jen Vescio (Partnerships), Matt Jasie (Finance), Hannes Rudolph (Community), and Kelly Pike (Talent).

## Partners: OpenAI and NVIDIA

The foundation has secured two marquee partners:

- **OpenAI** has committed to inference support, donated to the foundation, and stood up Claw Labs — a team inside OpenAI led by Steinberger working on shared product improvements.
- **NVIDIA** launched **NeMoClaw** at GTC: a one-command install of OpenClaw with open weights and optimized inference on NVIDIA hardware.

## Neutral Ground for the Agentic Era

The foundation's ambition extends beyond OpenClaw itself. Its goal is to be "the Switzerland of AI" — neutral ground where every model and lab can collaborate on agent standards. Foundation-convened councils are already underway on agent identity, agent profiles, evals, and enterprise deployment.

Open roles remain across engineering, product design, developer relations, and marketing.
